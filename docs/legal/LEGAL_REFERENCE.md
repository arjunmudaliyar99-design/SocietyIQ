# SocietyIQ — Legal reference for the founders

A plain-language guide to the laws that govern Maharashtra housing societies and our own business. Researched 23 October 2026 from professional newsletters, consultant and law-firm articles and news reports, listed in §9. **This is not legal advice.** Laws and their dates must be checked against the official texts and by a lawyer or the society consultant before we rely on them in the product or in marketing.

---

## 1. The big picture

Maharashtra housing societies are governed by four layers. When they conflict, the higher layer wins.

1. **The Act:** Maharashtra Co-operative Societies Act, 1960 (MCS Act). Since 2019 it has a separate **Chapter XIII-B (sections 154B to 154B-31)** only for housing societies.
2. **The Rules:** Maharashtra Co-operative Societies Rules, 1961. On **18 June 2026** the state notified the **Amendment Rules, 2026**, adding **Chapter XI-B (Rules 106C-1 to 106C-14)**, the first rulebook made only for housing societies (published in the Gazette dated 22 June 2026, per one source).
3. **Government orders and directions** under section 79A (for example the 2001 order capping non-occupancy charges).
4. **The society's bye-laws:** most societies use the **Model Bye-laws 2014**. A rewritten **draft Model Bye-laws 2026** was circulated; public objections closed on 27 August 2026 and a rollout was targeted for October 2026. Until it is formally notified, each society's registered bye-laws remain binding.

About 1.27 lakh cooperative housing societies are registered in Maharashtra, about 33,200 of them in Greater Mumbai (quoted by the president of the Mumbai District Co-operative Housing Federation, Oct 2026).

## 3. Charges: what the 2026 Rules say (Rule 106C-12, 106C-11)

**Permitted charge heads:** service charges, property tax, water, lift, parking, interest on defaults, loan instalments and interest, non-occupancy, insurance, lease rent, non-agricultural tax, fund contributions, and other charges approved by the general body that are consistent with the Act and Rules.

**Service charges include:** staff salaries, society office costs, printing and postage, travel and conveyance, committee sitting fees, federation subscriptions, audit fees, meeting expenses, legal and retainer fees, and common electricity.

**How each charge must be shared:**

| Charge | Shared how |
|---|---|
| Service charges | Equally per flat, whatever the flat size |
| Property tax | As fixed by the municipal body for each flat; common-area tax by carpet area |
| Water | By number and size of taps or inlets in the sanctioned plan |
| Lift | Equally among flats in the building that has the lift |
| Parking | At the rate fixed by the general body |
| Interest on late dues | General body rate, **maximum 12% simple interest per year** (was 21%) |
| Non-occupancy | **10% of service charges** (not 10% of the whole bill) |
| Insurance, lease rent | By carpet area |
| Major repairs | By carpet area, as approved by the general body |
| Sinking fund | **At least 0.25% per year** of each flat's architect-certified original construction cost |
| Repair and maintenance fund | **At least 0.75% per year** of the same |
| Election fund | Equally |
| Welfare fund | Voluntary |
| Education and training | ₹10 per member per month (Rule 106C-7) |

**Parking (the advisor's question):** parking is charged at the rate the general body fixes. The draft Model Bye-laws 2026 say open and stilt parking in the approved layout cannot ordinarily be sold or permanently transferred; it is allotted. Allotment rules (who gets a slot, waiting list, second vehicle) come from the society's bye-laws. Confirm with the final bye-laws once notified.

**Owner vs tenant (non-occupancy, the advisor's question):** when a member lets the flat out, the society may levy non-occupancy charges of 10% of service charges, payable by the **member (owner)**. It is not charged if the flat is occupied by the member's family as defined in the bye-laws. The tenant has no obligation to the society for this charge; the owner and tenant may agree privately.

**Committee spending limit on repairs** without general body approval (Rule 106C-13): ₹1 lakh (up to 25 members), ₹2 lakh (26–50), ₹3 lakh (51–100), ₹4 lakh (101–1,000), ₹5 lakh (1,001+).

## 4. Property tax: how it is shared (the advisor's question)

**Short answer:** the society does not divide the building's property tax by rooms or by a formula of its own. Each flat pays **what the municipal corporation fixed for that flat** (Rule 106C-12). Only the common-area portion, if billed separately, is shared by carpet area.

**Mumbai (MCGM):**

- Tax is based on the **capital value** of the property: base value (ready reckoner rate) × carpet area × factors for building type, age, floor and use; then tax rates are applied as a percentage of capital value.
- **Residential properties up to 500 sq ft carpet area are exempt** from 1 January 2022. Some sources say the exemption covers all components; others say only the general (core) tax. Some sources mention a 60% concession for 501–700 sq ft, but one recent source describes it as only proposed. **Check the actual MCGM bill for each flat.**
- Payment is due by 30 June each year with a penalty after that, per one 2026 guide (verify).

**Your example:**

| Flat | Carpet area | MCGM property tax (if residential) | How SocietyIQ bills it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 room + kitchen (1RK) | 300 sq ft | Exempt (≤500 sq ft) | ₹0, reason "MCGM ≤500 sq ft exemption" |
| 2 rooms + kitchen | 500 sq ft | Exempt (≤500 sq ft) | ₹0, same reason |
| 2BHK | 650 sq ft | As assessed by MCGM (concession status to verify) | Exact amount from the MCGM bill |

Number of rooms does not matter; **carpet area and the municipal assessment** do. Outside Mumbai (Thane, Navi Mumbai, Pune), each corporation has its own rules; always use their bills.

## 5. Other points from the Legal & Society Advisor

- **Notices by SMS:** research did not yet confirm the exact provision that makes SMS a valid statutory notice. The 2026 Rules allow hybrid (online) meetings; recovery proceedings specify hand delivery, registered post A.D. or newspaper publication; some sources say email or messaging notices are valid where bye-laws allow, with proof. **Ask the advisor for the exact section or circular** before we use this in marketing (Q-012).
- **Nomination:** each member and each joint member files a separate nomination for their share during their lifetime; the society records it in the register (Rules 106C-8, 106C-9). On death, the nominee can become a provisional member (Form Y-4 with an indemnity bond); provisional members get no ownership or share certificate until the legal transfer is complete.
- **Recovery of dues:** application in Form Y-6 under section 154B-29 with ₹100 court fee; the Registrar issues a recovery certificate in Form Y-7, enforceable like land revenue arrears.

## 6. Our business structure (founders' question)

**What research found about costs in Maharashtra (2026 estimates from registration service websites; get a CA's quote):**

| Structure | Approx. total setup cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Sole proprietorship | Low (GST, Udyam, shop registration as needed) | One owner; owner personally liable; cannot issue shares to partners or investors |
| Partnership firm | about ₹5,500 – ₹14,500 | **Registration is compulsory in Maharashtra**; stamp duty ₹500 for capital up to ₹50,000; certified Marathi translation of the deed needed |
| LLP | about ₹8,000 – ₹25,000 | Limited liability; annual filings |
| Private Limited company | about ₹15,000 – ₹30,000 | Needed when raising investment |

So a registered partnership may cost about the same as the ₹10,000 you estimated for a proprietorship plus MOU, and less than the ₹25,000–40,000 you were quoted.

**Important caution about "proprietorship + MOU naming partners":** under the Indian Partnership Act, 1932, a partnership is the relation between people who agree to share the profits of a business carried on by all or any of them acting for all. An MOU that shares profits and names people as "partners" could legally create a partnership anyway, which in Maharashtra must be registered. Two cleaner options to discuss with a CA or lawyer:

1. **Register a partnership firm now** (low cost, honest structure), convert to a Private Limited company when investors come in; or
2. **Proprietorship in the Founder's name + a founders' agreement** that does **not** share profits now, but records each person's role, time commitment, a fixed fee or commission if any, a promise of equity with vesting (for example over 4 years with a 1-year cliff) when a Private Limited company is formed, confidentiality, and assignment of all code, designs and the brand to the business.

Either way, the agreement must cover: roles (the Founder and the Co-founder (Engineering): development; the Design & Marketing Lead: design and marketing), decision-making, what happens if someone leaves, IP ownership, non-solicitation of clients, and dispute resolution. Have it drafted or reviewed by a lawyer and signed by all.
