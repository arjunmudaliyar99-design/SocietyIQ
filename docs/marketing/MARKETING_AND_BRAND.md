## Part A — Brand book

### A1. Who we are

SocietyIQ automates the routine office work of a housing society: bills, dues, receipts, reminders, compliance dates and notices. It is built for committee members aged 50 to 70 and works in English, Marathi, Hindi, Gujarati and Tamil.

### A2. Taglines

| Use | Line |
|---|---|
| Main one-liner | **Society management, automated.** |
| Supporting line | Bills, dues, receipts and reminders run themselves, in your language. |
| Rules angle (2026) | Billing that follows the new housing society Rules, automatically. |
| Elderly angle | Big buttons. Your language. We set it up for you. |
| Resident angle | Pay your society dues in one tap. Get your receipt on your phone. |
| Investor line | SocietyIQ automates the back office of India's housing societies, starting with Maharashtra. |

Retired: "Your society's office, on autopilot" (too close to a competitor), "Know where your society's money is going" (older positioning).

Trademark and web check for the main one-liner before printing (Q-022).

### A3. Logo

- Files: `reference/brand/` only. Never redraw or recolour.
- **Mark:** teal rounded square with three rising buildings that double as a bar chart, an orange dot making the "i".
- **Light logo** on white or paper backgrounds; **dark logo** on `#17262E`.
- Clear space around the logo: at least the height of the orange dot on every side.
- Minimum size: mark 24 px on screen, 10 mm in print.
- Don't: stretch, add shadows, place on photos without a solid panel, change the "IQ" colour.

### A4. Colours

| Name | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Ink | `#17262E` | Text, dark backgrounds |
| Paper | `#F7F6F2` | Page backgrounds |
| Teal | `#2E6B5E` | Brand colour, buttons, headings |
| Mint | `#7FC2AE` | Accents on dark backgrounds |
| Orange | `#E39A6B` | The dot; small highlights only |
| Terracotta | `#C4622D` | Callouts, warnings |

Text must always be readable: dark text on light colours, white text only on Ink or Teal.

### A5. Typography

- Digital and print: **Noto Sans** family (covers Marathi, Hindi, Gujarati and Tamil scripts consistently).
- Headlines may use **Source Serif 4** in English decks for a classic feel (as in earlier decks); body text always Noto Sans.
- Minimum body size: 18 px on screen, 11 pt in print. Older readers first.

### A6. Voice and tone

- Respectful, calm, clear. Like a helpful, educated neighbour, not a salesperson.
- Short sentences. Plain words. Explain any term once (AMC = annual maintenance contract).
- Talk about outcomes: less work, fewer missed dates, clear money, fewer disputes.
- Don't lead with "AI". Mention it only as "reads the bill for you".

**Never say:** "guaranteed savings", "100% legal", "fraud", "overcharging", "monopoly", anything about competitors by name in ads, any legal claim not confirmed in `08` (for example "SMS notices are legally valid" until Q-012 is answered).

### A7. Imagery

- Real-looking Mumbai and Thane buildings, older committee members, phones with large text. Use licensed stock or our own photos with consent.
- Screens shown must use demo data with a "Demo data" tag.
- No copyrighted characters, logos of other apps, or photos of real residents without written consent.

## Part B — Marketing plan

### B1. Goal for the next stage

From the build plan and roadmap: research 50–60 buildings, sign 5–10 pilot societies, then 3 paying societies. Marketing exists to fill that funnel, not to chase downloads.

### B2. Who we talk to

| Person | What they care about | Lead message |
|---|---|---|
| Secretary (55–70) | Less paperwork, not being blamed, no missed deadlines | "Bills, receipts and reminders go out by themselves. You just approve." |
| Treasurer | Accurate books, audit, cash and cheques | "Every cash and cheque payment recorded in one tap, with a receipt and a clean ledger for the auditor." |
| Chairman and committee | Peaceful AGMs, following the law | "Billing that follows the 2026 housing society Rules, so members have fewer reasons to dispute." |
| Residents | Easy payment, transparency | "Pay in one tap, get your receipt on your phone, see notices first." |
| Society consultants and accountants | Serving more societies with less effort | "Manage all your client societies from one place." |
| Gujarati-majority societies | Value for money, negotiation | Lead with rupees and hours saved; annual prepay discount |

### B3. The timely hook: the 2026 Rules

From 18 June 2026, Maharashtra's housing-society Rules fix how charges are shared (Rule 106C-12): service charges equal per flat, interest on late dues at most 12% simple, non-occupancy charges 10% of service charges, water by number of taps, insurance by carpet area. Many societies still bill the old way.

Content ideas:
- "Is your society's bill following the 2026 Rules? 5 things to check" (post, PDF, 60-second video in Marathi, Hindi, Gujarati).
- Free "bill check" for a society: we compare their current bill format with Rule 106C-12 and show what changes. This doubles as field research and a lead.
- Always add: "General information, not legal advice. Check with your society's legal adviser."

### B4. Channels (in order of priority, low budget first)

1. **Society consultants and accountants** who handle many societies (the strongest channel).
2. **Federations and associations:** Mumbai District Co-operative Housing Federation, Maharashtra State Co-operative Housing Federation, Maharashtra Societies Welfare Association; offer short sessions on the 2026 Rules and simple billing. Meet the people the advisor referred (Q-023).
3. **Field visits** in one cluster (Thane and nearby Mumbai suburbs), using the field kit.
4. **Word of mouth and referrals:** one free month for each society that refers another.
5. **WhatsApp:** a business number for enquiries and support; share short videos and PDFs into society groups only when invited.
6. **Short videos** in Marathi, Hindi, Gujarati (and English): how to read your maintenance bill, AGM notice mistakes, fire audit deadlines, the 2026 Rules.
7. **Website and Google Business profile:** one page, demo video, pilot form.
8. Later: local newspaper features, society magazines, paid ads once a paying society exists.

**Future partner marketing (after 10–15 societies and business registration):** bundled services with Supplybase (plumbers, painters, technicians) and a fire-safety equipment company, plus legal and CA referrals. Do not advertise these yet.

### B5. Field kit (to make)

1. One-page leaflet (English + Marathi on the back; Hindi and Gujarati versions).
2. 10-question interview sheet (current tools, who makes bills, cash/cheque share, biggest pain, interest in a pilot, decision maker, budget).
3. Pilot letter (one page).
4. Demo on a phone and a laptop (demo data).
5. Contact sheet to log each visit (see B6).

### B6. Data to collect from every conversation (for the CRM sheet)

Society name, area, number of flats, wings and lifts, current billing method (paper / Excel / Tally / app name), who prepares bills, share of cash and cheque payments, biggest problem in their words, decision maker's name and role, languages spoken, interest level (1–5), next step and date, referral source. Store with consent; no residents' personal data.

### B7. Pilot offer (current)

3 months free, "free with commitment": a signed one-page pilot letter, a named champion, a 30-minute monthly feedback call, and a price discussion in month 3. We set up their records.

### B8. Pricing language (until validated)

"Pilot societies pay nothing for 3 months. After that, plans are expected to start around ₹1,499 a month, depending on society size." Do not publish final prices.

### B9. What to measure

Conversations held, societies interested (4–5 on the 1–5 scale), pilot letters signed, pilots active weekly, pilots converted to paid, referrals received, content views by language.
